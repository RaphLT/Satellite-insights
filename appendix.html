  • Code: github.com/RaphLT/Satellite-insights

B. What works, what does not, what it was tested on, who did what

Main prototype: Car Counter from Above (question A). The tool runs in any recent browser without a login or installation. The AI model runs inside the browser, and photos are not uploaded anywhere. You load an aerial photo, or one of three IGN samples of Auchan Englos (2018, 2021, 2025). You set the ground size of one pixel and can draw a zone, such as Auchan's car parks. The tool circles each car it finds, counts them, and compares two photos as a percentage change: the kind of signal Orbital Insight sold. It checks its own scale with the median detection size and suggests a fix if that looks wrong. Two models are available: ours, trained on 515 hand-labelled COWC cars, and a ready-made nano model trained on DOTA. We tested it on COWC Potsdam tile 2_12 (113 hand-marked cars, never used for training). There, our model got 108 right with 16 false alarms and 5 misses, and the ready-made nano model got 104 right, 10 false alarms, 9 misses. On one Englos area of about 100 cars counted by eye, ours counted 95. On the samples, the browser's counts were within about 5% of our Python copy of the same method. What does not work well: the ready-made nano model misses many cars on the softer IGN photos, so ours is the default. Photos coarser than about 25 cm per pixel, such as IGN before 2012 at 50 cm, are too coarse. The tool counts every car, including cars on roads, staff cars and dealer stock. Comparisons between two IGN photos are unreliable because IGN does not publish the day each photo was flown. The 2021 sample shows a nearly empty car park, perhaps a Sunday, and the tool reports +427% for 2025. The tool explains this on the page.

Prototype 2 (ready-made detector, supporting work). It runs in Colab or on a laptop with free tools only, and we trained nothing for it. On COWC tile 2_12 the medium DOTA model found 106 of 113 cars with 11 false alarms (94% found, 91% right). On the IGN Englos photo it counted 2,335 cars, and on the hand-checked area it found about 93 of ~103 with about 2 false alarms. It needs ×5 zoom plus sharpening on IGN, and sharpening hurts on COWC. Correction: earlier versions reported 92 right and 2,217 cars because of the colour-order bug (row 22).

Prototype 3 (training our own model, supporting work). A small model that had never seen an aerial image learned from 515 hand-labelled cars in about an hour on a CPU. With the cut-off chosen on validation tile 6_8 (0.55), it found 102 of 113 cars on the unseen tile 2_12 with 4 false alarms (90% found, 96% right). The ready-made medium model scored 94% and 91%. At that cut-off it found only 69 cars in the Englos area (~100 there), so it has to be re-tuned for French photos; at 0.25 it found about 95. The test tile comes from the same city and camera as the training tiles, which favours our model. Correction: an earlier version reported 65% found because of the colour-order bug.

Prototype 4 (Englos over time, unfinished, not on the professor's list). The 2004 and 2009 photos (50 cm per pixel) are too coarse to count. The counts made earlier for 2004, 2009 and 2015 came before the colour-order fix and have been withdrawn. With the browser tool, Auchan's car parks hold 166 cars in 2021 and 875 in 2025 (+427%); 2015 and 2018 are still to be counted. IGN does not say which of several days each photo was taken on, and one candidate (8 May 2018) is a public holiday, so year-to-year changes cannot be read as changes in Auchan's business.

Prototype 1 (signal decay).

The prototype runs in any browser without a login and in Google Colab. For each set of assumptions, it computes the number of client funds that maximises a data vendor's revenue, the price that number implies, and the revenue at the price the article reports. The web page and the notebook give identical results. The notebook checks its own arithmetic: the exclusive-fund profit equals 4.76% × capital × 4, and under the "fixed pie" curve total profit stays the same for any number of funds. It does not use satellite images or any Orbital Insight sales data. It is a toy model whose central input, how fast a fund's edge shrinks as more funds buy the signal, is unknown, so we show four curves instead of one answer. Its revenues are upper bounds, because we assume the vendor captures all of each fund's profit. It also covers only the retail car-count product. We tested it on real inputs from Katona et al. (2025): the 4.76% three-day return in Table 3 and the "hundreds of thousands" price on p. 556. We also used Orbital Insight's $130M of funding and $9.5M of expected 2023 revenue (Poulson, 2023). Capital per fund, processing cost, the exact price and the value of a non-rival client are our assumptions, and the sliders let readers change them.
