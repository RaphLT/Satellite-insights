Supporting prototype 1 · The business

How a data signal loses value as more funds buy it

A toy market in which one vendor, like Orbital Insight, sells the same parking-lot signal to k hedge funds. The more funds trade on it, the less each one earns from it. Move the sliders to change our assumptions; the article's figures stay fixed.

The decision this prototype supports: how many funds should a vendor sell its signal to, at what price, and can that business ever carry the ~$130M of venture capital Orbital Insight raised? And would a new entrant do better selling to customers who don't need exclusivity?

What the baseline shows

With the default sliders, the three curves that fall fast enough reach the reported price at 7–13 clients, which gives the vendor $2–6M a year. The "slow" curve looks better on paper (about $68M a year with 50 clients), but only because it charges each fund far more than funds actually paid: its price never falls to the reported range, so it does not fit the market the article describes. Chart 4 shows the way out: customers who do not compete with each other keep paying as the vendor adds more of them.

The four curves

Slow 1/(1+0.1(k−1)): the edge fades gently. Fixed pie 1/k: a fixed amount of mispricing split equally. Competition 4/(k+1)²: funds race to trade first and together earn less than one exclusive fund would (Cournot competition with linear price impact). Fast e−0.3(k−1): the edge collapses after a few clients. Price per client = that fund's yearly trading profit minus its own processing cost, assuming the vendor captures the whole surplus, so all revenues are upper bounds.

Limits

This is a toy model, not a forecast. It uses no satellite images and no Orbital Insight sales data. The 4.76% comes from RS Metrics data for 2011–2017, when some funds already had the signal, so the "exclusive" edge is if anything understated. It models one product (retail car counts), not Orbital Insight's oil or location-data products. The same model is in a Python notebook (prototypes/1_signal_decay/signal_decay_simulation.ipynb), which gives identical numbers and checks its own arithmetic.
