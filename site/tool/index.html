Car Counter from Above

Group 7 · AI for Business & Society, SKEMA Lille. The decision this tool supports: could a student rebuild the signal Orbital Insight sold to hedge funds, the change in cars parked at a retailer from one photo to the next, using only free images and free AI? Load an aerial photo and the AI counts the cars inside your browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere.

AI model:
Count two photos of the same car park to see the change, the number Orbital Insight sold.

How to use it

1. Pick a sample (Auchan Englos near Lille, free IGN aerial photos) or drop your own aerial photo. 2. Set the ground size of one pixel in metres; the samples fill it in. 3. Optionally drag a rectangle on the photo to count one zone only (for the samples, “Auchan car parks” draws the store's own lots). 4. Press Count. A blue ring marks each car found. Check the “median detection size”: it should be close to the value shown (real cars are about 4.5 m; our model draws slightly bigger boxes). If it is far off, your metres-per-pixel is probably wrong; press the suggested fix and count again.

Your own photo: on geoportail.gouv.fr take a screenshot of a car park zoomed in close, and measure a known distance with the ruler tool to work out metres per pixel. Or download an exact image from IGN's free WMS service: for a box W metres wide saved at P pixels, metres per pixel = W / P.

On the Englos photos the two models disagree more than on Potsdam: the ready-made nano model misses many cars on the softer French photos, which is why ours is the default.

Limits

Works best on sharp, top-down photos with cars about 20 px long or more (25 cm per pixel or better); free photos of 50 cm per pixel (IGN before 2012) are too coarse. It counts every car in the area, on roads too, unless you draw a zone. It cannot tell a customer from an employee or a car-dealer's stock. Two photos are only comparable if taken at a similar time of day and day of week: IGN does not say on which day each photo was flown, and the 2021 candidates include a Sunday, when the store is closed. Orbital Insight bought near-daily paid images for exactly this reason. Tested on one COWC tile and one hand-checked IGN area.
