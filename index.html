Was selling AI-processed satellite data to investors a viable business?

The case is Orbital Insight (Palo Alto, founded 2013, sold to Privateer in May 2024). It counted cars in retailers' parking lots from satellite images and sold the counts to hedge funds, so they could guess a retailer's sales before the official figures came out. Our main source is Katona, Painter, Patatoukas & Zeng (2025), On the capital market consequences of big data: Evidence from outer space, JFQA 60(2).

Our answer in two sentences. The car-count signal was worth most while few funds had it, and it lost value as it spread, so a business built on selling exclusive data could not carry the ~$130M of venture capital Orbital Insight raised (its expected 2023 revenue was ~$9.5M). A new entrant needs customers who do not need exclusivity, because the AI itself is now close to free.

Our prototype: Car Counter from Above

Main prototype · the professor's question A

Count the cars in a car park, in your browser →

Load a free IGN aerial photo of the Auchan Englos retail park near Lille (2018, 2021 or 2025), or your own photo. The AI counts the cars inside your browser, nothing is uploaded, and it compares two photos as a percentage change: the kind of number Orbital Insight sold to hedge funds.

Our own model, trained on 515 hand-marked cars, got 108 of 113 cars right on a test photo it had never seen (16 false alarms). Comparing 2021 and 2025 in Auchan's car parks gives +427%, most likely because of the day each photo was taken rather than Auchan's business. That gap is our argument in one number.

The tool needs a recent browser and downloads about 22 MB of AI model the first time you count. Counting a whole photo takes from a few seconds to a few minutes, depending on your computer.

Supporting work: four steps of the argument

Each step tests one part of Orbital Insight's business with real data from our case. Everything was built by "vibe-coding": an AI assistant wrote the code, and we directed it and checked the results. Every prompt is in the appendix.

1 · The business

How a data signal loses value

An interactive toy market: one vendor sells the same signal to more and more funds. How many clients, at what price?

At a price in the range the article reports, three of four curves imply 7–13 clients and $2–6M of yearly revenue, far below what $130M of funding needs.
2 · The technology

Can a student rebuild the car counter?

A free, ready-made detector, tested on cars marked by hand and on a free photo of Auchan Englos.

It found 94% of hand-marked cars, and 91% of what it counted were real cars. 2,335 cars counted at Englos.
3 · The data behind the AI

Training our own model

We trained a small detector ourselves on 515 hand-marked cars, in about an hour on a laptop processor.

It found 90% of cars with 96% right, matching the ready-made model on similar photos. But it had to be re-tuned for French photos.
