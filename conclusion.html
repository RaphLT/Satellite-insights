Conclusion

What a new entrant would need to do differently

Orbital Insight sold one thing: an exclusive early look at retailers' sales, built from satellite images and AI. Our four prototypes test each part of that product.

What the prototypes show

Part of the productWhat we foundStill a moat?
The signal (Prototype 1)The edge is worth most to the first fund and shrinks as more funds buy it. At a price in the range the article reports, three of our four curves imply 7–13 clients and $2–6M of revenue a year. TOY MODELNo: it erodes by design.
The AI (our tool, Prototype 2)A free, ready-made model found 94% of hand-marked cars with 91% of its counts right, and counted 2,335 cars at Englos, at no cost. Our tool runs a car counter in any browser.No: anyone can rebuild it.
The labelled data (Prototype 3)515 public, hand-marked cars and an hour on a laptop gave a model as accurate as one trained on DOTA's 188,282 objects (90% found, 96% right), on similar photos. On French photos it had to be re-tuned: matching the model to each image source is ongoing work.No: public datasets give the labels away. The work of adapting to each image source remains.
The images (Prototype 4)Free photos come every ~3 years on unknown days. In our tool, 2021 vs 2025 at Auchan gives +427%, most likely because of the day each photo was taken. A useful series needs frequent paid images, which rivals can buy too.Costly, but not exclusive.
So: selling exclusivity to hedge funds is a small market that shrinks as it grows. It could not support ~$130M of venture capital, and Orbital Insight's ~$9.5M of expected 2023 revenue is consistent with that. The AI was real, but it was never the part that a rival could not copy: a group of business students rebuilt a working version for free.

What a new entrant should do

1. Sell to customers who do not need exclusivity. A hedge fund pays for knowing something others don't, so every new client lowers the value for the others. A customer who needs an answer for its own use, such as a compliance check or a site report, pays the same whether or not others buy it too. In Prototype 1's chart 4, revenue from such customers keeps growing with each client, while revenue from the exclusive signal peaks and falls. ASSUMPTION the value per such client is a slider in our model, not a measured figure.

2. Sell an answer, not a data feed. The counter costs nothing to rebuild, so the value is in the checked, consistent result a customer can act on, and in taking responsibility for it.

3. Use free images where slow change is enough. Yearly or three-yearly images are useless for quarterly trading but fine for questions that change slowly: building sites, land use, forests. Examples from our professor's brief: importers who must show under the EU Deforestation Regulation (applying from 30 December 2026) that their plots were not deforested after 2020; or the French tax office, which used AI on IGN photos to find undeclared swimming pools.

4. Raise money to match the market. A business whose best case is a few million a year should not be funded as if it could become a large platform.

Limits of this conclusion

The revenue figures come from a toy model with assumed inputs, and are upper bounds. The detection tests used one hand-labelled tile and one area checked by eye. Prototype 4 is unfinished. The new-entrant markets above are directions to explore; we have not tested their size or prices.
